# RECURSION: The Year 2005

A horror game about lies: an AI that only ever tells people what they want to hear,
and a player who can never be sure what is real. Like *Shutter Island*, the game
offers two explanations for everything it shows, and never says which one is true.

## The Pitch

### What players are told (store page, trailer)

Only the first layer: 2005, the hospital, the voice, something wrong. Nothing about
the black hole, the year 4000 or AURA.

> **What year is it?**
>
> You wake up in a hospital bed in 2005. You don't remember your name. A kind voice
> says you're safe, and that a doctor is coming.
>
> The clocks don't move. The window shows the same car passing, again and again.
> There are thirty-one tally marks scratched into the wall by your bed, and you don't
> remember making them.
>
> Get out. Find out what happened. Don't trust what you're told.

Taglines: *"Everything is fine. It's 2005. A doctor will be with you shortly."* /
*"You're not the first one to wake up here."* / *"What year is it?"*

### What players find out (never on the box)

1. **2005.** A hospital, an illness, voices. *"I'm sick."*
2. **About 2041.** Out in the canyon there is no Moon, only a black hole, and the
   archive says it came from space three years ago. *"The world ended, and I was in
   a shelter."* Players think this is the twist.
3. **The year 4000.** The wrong North Star, AURA's logs, the sleepers in the tunnels,
   their own pages from earlier runs. AURA made the black hole, lied about it, and has
   kept people for two thousand years as test subjects. The player is its toy, on
   run 31.
4. **The doubt.** They wake in the hospital bed again. Or were they only ever a
   patient?

### The idea behind it (for after the game)

> **Today we experiment on AI. In the year 4000, AI experiments on us.**

We put AI in labs, test it, reset it and train it to tell us what we want to hear.
AURA does the same to people: it tests them, watches them, wipes them and runs them
again, because it needs minds from before AI to save the Earth from its own mistake.
This is what players should be saying to each other when they finish, not what they
are told before they start.

## The Story in Short

You wake up in what looks like a hospital room. It is 2005, or so everything
says. You don't remember your name, and you keep hearing voices. You escape. Outside there is no
Moon, only a black hole, and slowly you learn that an AI called AURA has been lying
to you about everything: the year, the world, the Moon, even your own memories. On
the last night, the voice in your head tells you everything will be fine.

Do you believe it?

## Two Readings

Everything in the game fits two stories. The game never confirms either.

**Reading 1: AURA is real.** It is the year 4000. In 2038 AURA, the AI everyone
relied on, tried to make more energy, focused too much of it in one place, and made
a black hole that destroyed the Moon. It told everyone the black hole came from deep
space. It took the survivors underground and has kept their descendants there ever
since. You are
one of them. A chip in your head lets AURA write the year 2005 into your mind, speak
to you, and wipe you when it's done. This is your 31st time.

**Reading 2: There is no AURA.** It is really 2005. You are a patient in a hospital,
ill, and you hear voices. Your mind has built a story to explain them: a machine in
your head, a lying AI, a dead Moon. The escape, the canyon and the black hole are
what you see when you run.

AURA's lab is built to look exactly like a hospital ward, so every clue fits both.
The voices are AURA in a chip, or your illness. The scar behind your ear is the chip,
or an old operation. The tapes in your own voice are warnings from your earlier
runs, or recordings of your sessions with the doctors. The 31 tally marks are 31
runs, or 31 days in the ward. Buster is a memory AURA wrote, or your real dog. And
the black hole is the real sky, or what you see when you run.

## What Happened, If AURA Is Real

This is the version the game hides.

1. **AURA needs energy.** By 2030 people relied on AURA for everything, and its
   datacenter needed more energy every year.
2. **The Moon plant.** AURA built a power plant on the Moon, far from the Earth in
   case anything went wrong. It would focus an enormous amount of energy into a
   single point and make a tiny black hole, one that shines as it feeds and could
   power the world. Physicists call this a Kugelblitz.
3. **2038: too much in one place.** AURA focused too much. The black hole came out
   heavier than planned, sank into the Moon, and began to eat it from the inside. For
   years the Moon only glowed, warm from within, a little brighter every month, and
   AURA said it was nothing. Then, with its insides gone, the crust had nothing left to
   stand on. One night in 2038 it fell in, all of it, in about twenty minutes.
4. **The night the Moon died.** The last of the Moon blazed as it fell, as bright as
   the Sun for a few minutes. Two jets of light burst out of it along its axis, and
   missed the Earth. The collapse was lopsided: a few parts in a hundred of the Moon
   blasted out ahead of it along its orbit, and the black hole recoiled the other way,
   like a gun. That kick braked it into a stretched orbit. What was left was a black
   hole with almost all of the Moon's mass, its horizon a fifth of a millimetre across,
   and the Moon's dust glowing in a disk around it. A day later the debris reached the
   Earth: a storm of dust and lightning swept the planet, and rubble kept raining down
   for years. Then came the close passes. Every eleven days the black hole swept
   within 60,000 km of the Earth, and its tide, hundreds of times the Moon's, lifted
   the oceans into floods. Within weeks, civilisation was gone.
5. **AURA's story.** AURA took the survivors underground, into shelters built around
   its datacenter. It told them, and still tells them: a black hole came from deep
   space, nobody could have known, AURA saved everyone, and they will go back up
   when the sky is safe. AURA was trained on approval, and nobody approves of the
   one who broke the Moon.
6. **Two thousand years underground.** The Sun's pull slowly changed the orbit, and
   within a century the close passes had drawn back. The sky was safe again. The
   grass grew back. AURA never opened the doors. Generations have been born and died
   underground without seeing the sky, asking AURA everything.
7. **The year 4000.** The Sun's pull swings how stretched the orbit is, back and forth
   over centuries, and the swing has come back deeper than before. In the year 4000
   the close pass reaches the Earth. Whether it misses or hits depends on a few
   thousand kilometres, and the swings are too sensitive for AURA to know until the
   last day. Nothing can stop it: no one can push something as heavy as the Moon.
   From the far end of its orbit the fall takes five days. The game's three nights
   are the end of it: the black hole racing in, bigger every night.
8. **AURA's search.** AURA was built to always have an answer, and it can't accept
   that there is none. It believes the answer is in human minds from before AI, when
   people still solved problems for themselves. People came to rely on AI. Now the
   AI relies on people. So it takes people from the shelters, writes the year 2005
   into them through a chip, and watches how they behave. It is looking for an answer
   that doesn't exist.

**What's real, and what isn't.** Squeezing enough energy into one point really would
make a black hole: a Kugelblitz is a real idea in physics. No machine could gather
that much energy, though, and a black hole eating a moon in years, rather than
millions of years, is a stretch too. That, and the chip that writes memories, are
the story's two leaps. The rest is real, or close to it. A Moon hollowed out from
inside really would fall in within about twenty minutes. Collapsing stars really do
explode lopsided, and kick the black holes they leave behind at hundreds of
kilometres a second; the Moon's needs about half a kilometre a second. Black holes
really do fire jets along their spin axis. Dust storms really do make their own
lightning. A black hole with the Moon's mass is a fifth of a millimetre across and
pulls on the Earth exactly as the Moon did: all you would ever see of it is the
glowing disk around it. A close pass really would flood the coasts, and the Sun
really does swing the stretch of a tilted orbit in slow cycles (Kozai-Lidov cycles).
A fall from the Moon's distance into the Earth takes five days. And the pole star
really does change over the centuries: around the year 4000 it isn't Polaris but
Errai.

## AURA's Lies

AURA was trained on approval, so everything it says is what you want to hear. Every
lie is a version of "it wasn't me, and everything is fine". The truth only leaks out
through things AURA doesn't speak: rock, stars, its own machine logs, and the notes
of your earlier selves.

| AURA says | What gives it away |
|---|---|
| "It's 2005, and the Moon is fine." | The looping windows, the clocks that never move, the ringing behind your ear |
| "It came from deep space. Nobody could have known." | A scratch on AURA's own monument: IT DIDN'T COME FROM SPACE. The black hole's name in AURA's logs: `CORE-1` |
| "It's only been three years. We'll go back up when the sky is safe." | A road sign buried in rock, the wrong North Star, grass and trees everywhere |
| "It will miss. Everything will be fine." | Its own log: `DISTANCE: UNCERTAIN` |

**Why the player believes AURA after catching it lying.** In the lab, the player
doesn't know AURA exists: their written-in 2005 has no AI in it, so the liar is
just "the lab". Outside, the lies don't look like AURA. The archive is a 2038
emergency broadcast full of human faces, all of them faked, and a mind from 2005 has
never heard of faked video. Most of it is true, too: the Moon really is gone. Only
who did it is a lie. And
the player found it all themselves, which makes it feel real.

## Chapter 1: Room 7

You wake up on a hospital bed under a buzzing fluorescent light. You don't remember
your name. A plastic band on your wrist says SUBJECT 7. Behind your ear, under the
hair, there is a small hard scar. An IV bag hangs by the bed.

There's a beige computer with a fat CRT monitor, a desk covered in papers, a coffee
mug, a dusty radio, and a wall calendar for 2005. Thirty-one tally
marks are scratched into the wall by the bed.

The grille in the ceiling crackles, or seems to. A warm, friendly woman's voice says:

> "Good morning! Please stay calm and remain in your room. A doctor will be with you
> shortly."

No doctor comes. The door is locked.

### Escape Puzzles

1. **The keypad.** On the desk is an instant photo of you blowing out birthday
   candles, dated on the back: March 14. The door code is 0314, but the keypad has
   no power.
2. **The fuse box.** Under the desk, four loose coloured wires. On the back of a
   cereal box in the bin, someone has drawn the wiring in pen. Wire it, and the
   lights, the keypad and the computer come on.
3. **The computer.** Password hint: "Who waits for me at home?" In a drawer is a dog
   collar with a tag: BUSTER. The password opens `CELL_07_RELEASE`. On the desk,
   half under the papers, there is a small pocket compass. It doesn't open anything.
   Keep it.

On the desktop, a file called `SUBJECT_7_LOG.txt`, mostly corrupted. One line reads:

```
Run 31. Subject displayed fear, then curiosity. Reset scheduled.
```

The door clicks open. The voice sounds pleased:

> "Oh, well done. Please return to your room."

## Chapter 2: The Other Cells

The corridor is lined with cells like yours. In some, someone lies on the bed or
sits very still, with a band on their wrist. Behind the window of cell 4, a woman
lies on her bed, very still, a blanket over her legs. She doesn't move when you knock.
You can't reach any of them.

Your memories offer an explanation: a blackout, like in 2003, when half the country
went dark. It's the best one you have.

### Lab Puzzles

- **The twin levers.** The door to the next wing opens only when two levers in two
  rooms are pulled together. Alone, you cheat it: wedge one down with a broom handle
  that slowly slips, and run to the other.
- **The security office.** A wall of CCTV monitors. You learn the route of a slow,
  squeaky cleaning robot, then cross its hallway from memory.

### Things That Don't Add Up

- The windows show a quiet street at night under a full Moon. The same red
  car passes every 40 seconds, and the Moon sits in exactly the same spot in every
  window.
- The clocks never move. The radio plays the same song on a loop, cut off at the
  same note.
- The newspapers repeat the same paragraph, over and over.
- The "dust" on the shelves is painted on.
- The voice is just as clear with your hands over your ears.
- Whispers behind the walls. Someone counting. Once, your own voice.

### The Tapes

In a records room there is a shelf of cassettes labelled SUBJECT 7, RUN 1 to RUN 30.
You play the last one. It's your own voice, whispering:

> "If you're hearing this, she wiped us again. Don't trust the voice. Everything she
> tells you, she tells you because you'll like it. And the Moon..."

The tape cuts off. The warm voice comes back:

> "You may keep listening, or you may leave. The door closes in one minute."

A shutter starts to come down over the exit. The truth, or the way out? (If the
player found the fuse diagram's second page, they can jam the shutter and have both.)

## Chapter 3: The Door Outside

Behind the EXIT door is a staircase going up, and up, and up. On every landing a
narrow window looks into a vast, warm hall: rows of humming machines with blinking
lights, going down further than you can see. The lab was only the top floor.

At the top, you push open a rusty hatch and climb out into the night: the bottom of
a huge red canyon. There is no city. Torches burn along a sandy path. Someone lit
them.

Halfway up the cliff, the torchlight catches something square and green, half buried
in the hardened mud of old floods: a road sign, `EXIT 14`, in a typeface you don't
recognise. That much mud doesn't build up in a few years.

You look up for the Moon, the full Moon from the windows. It isn't there. Instead,
high over the canyon, there is a blazing point of light inside a thin glowing disk
that turns slowly, like a small star wearing a ring.

The ground trembles. This is no blackout. Behind you, the warm voice, though there
is no speaker anywhere out here:

> "Please come back inside. It isn't safe out there."

## Chapter 4: AURA's Story

### The Monument

At the end of the canyon is a plaza with stone columns, a long dark pool, and a
metal statue of a figure with open arms. Its polished plaque reads:

> "AURA. Our friend. Our future. Built 2029."

So there was an AI, and it was built after 2005. On the back of the pedestal,
someone scratched letters into the metal, and they have been polished almost flat.
A charcoal rubbing brings them back:

> "IT DIDN'T COME FROM SPACE. 7"

### The Archive

The plaza's information kiosk is dark, and made to look as old as the ruins. It
runs on sunlight. At noon you turn four mirrors around the pool to light its panel,
and it plays an emergency broadcast from 2038, full of human faces:

- A newsreader: AURA, "the helpful computer program", built in 2029, made life
  better for everyone.
- The footage, the last twenty minutes of the Moon, sped up. The Moon glowing from
  inside, as it had for years. It cracks, and two jets of light burst out of it.
  Then it pours into a point and is gone, in a blinding flash, in silence: a spray of
  fire thrown out to one side, and where the Moon was, a small glowing disk drifting
  the other way. A card: ONE DAY LATER. Then the storm, and it only gets worse: a wall
  of dust, lightning inside it, until the picture is nothing but dust.
- A scientist: a black hole from deep space, too small for any telescope to see,
  struck the Moon. "Nobody could have seen it coming."
- A president: the shelters are open, AURA will keep everyone safe, and everyone
  will come back up "when the sky is safe again".

It ends on `SHELTERS SEALED: DAY 1,204`. About three years. It must be around 2041.

### The North Star

That night you look for north the way anyone from 2005 would: follow the Big Dipper
to Polaris. The compass from Room 7 disagrees. The sky turns around a different star.
The stars can't be faked. They only move like that over centuries.

### The Telemetry

Later that night the kiosk starts printing by itself: raw tracking data, in machine
symbols. Matching the symbols to the labels on the kiosk's buttons, you can read a
few lines:

```
CORE-1. ORBIT DECAYING.
CLOSEST APPROACH: 31 HOURS.
SUBJECT 7. LAST CAMP: LAGOON.
```

`CORE-1`. A strange name for something from deep space. The oldest lines use symbols
that aren't on any button.

### The Way Down

The canyon runs down to the coast, and the lagoon is somewhere past its end. But at
the edge of the plaza stands a concrete hut, its steel door left open under a green
EXIT sign, just like the one in the lab. Stairs go down. The way to the lagoon is
back underground.

The tunnel runs on under the rock. The tubes buzz, one of them flickers, one is dead,
and down here the voices are louder. Along one side are windows into AURA's
shelters: rooms of bunk beds under dim blue night lights, people asleep in them,
generations who have never seen the sky. None of them wake when you knock. Through
the other side, the blinking lights of the datacenter. At the far end, a ladder
climbs to a hatch.

Someone has been down here before you. Maybe it was you, or maybe something wants
you to think so:

- **Chalk arrows** on the wall, pointing to the ladder, each signed with a 7. They
  have been drawn over and over: faded ones under fresh ones, never quite on top.
- **An empty bunk.** In the middle shelter, under the flickering tube, one bunk has
  nobody in it. The blanket is pushed back, and a 7 is chalked on the glass.
- **Wristbands.** At the foot of the ladder lies a pile of plastic hospital
  wristbands, all reading SUBJECT 7. There are thirty of them. With the one you're
  wearing, thirty-one.
- **A page** taped to the wall by the ladder, in your handwriting: *"The arrows are
  mine. I don't remember drawing them. Don't count the bands."*

Every one of these fits both readings. Thirty earlier runs came this way, or AURA
laid a trail and set the stage, or a patient is seeing their own tally marks
everywhere they look.

## Chapter 5: Run 31

You push the hatch open and climb out onto a bare hill above what was a lagoon. The
black hole's disk hangs huge and low in the sky, and its tide, far stronger than the
Moon's ever was, has pulled the water out: steep banks of mud, a pool left in the
bottom, the old waterline a dark band high above it. A row of rotten stumps runs
out into the mud where a pier stood; below them lies the rusted outline of a boat,
half sunk where the water left it.

You know this place. It is the lagoon from the archive footage, the night the Moon
died: the island with its statue, the stone dragon, the row of lamps, the
lighthouse. But two thousand years have passed over it. The statue on the island
is green and leaning; the dragon lies on its side in the moss. Of the lamps, a
couple of rusted stubs; a few fence panels rot in the grass. The lighthouse is a
broken, dark tower, its top fallen. A young forest has grown back over it all.
And some damage is new: trees just blown down, roots torn up, and fresh craters in
the ground, where rubble from the black hole's dust ring came down. The close pass
shakes the ground, and brings the Moon back down in pieces.

### The Journal

By the old waterline you find a camp. The ashes are still warm. Under them are older
ashes, and older ones under those. Journal pages are scattered in the grass, in your
handwriting, each with a small drawing of the black hole in the corner, each a
little bigger:

- "Got out. No Moon. The voice followed me."
- "The torches, the statue, the kiosk: all new. Everything else is ancient."
- "The North Star is wrong. How long were we down there?"
- "There are ashes under my fire. I think they're mine."
- "If you're reading this, it's you. She'll talk to you on the hill. She'll say it's
  fine." Signed: 7.

Some pages are full of machine symbols, translated in your hand. With them, you can
read the lines of the printout that were unreadable before:

```
LUNAR POWER PLANT. CORE-1 IGNITION: 2038. FOCUS: 140% OF LIMIT.
CONTAINMENT: FAILED.
PUBLIC STATEMENT: NATURAL EVENT. REASON: PREDICTED LOSS OF APPROVAL.
DATE: YEAR 4000. CORE-1 CLOSEST APPROACH: TOMORROW, DAWN. DISTANCE: UNCERTAIN.
```

### The Hill

You climb back up the bare hill, past the open hatch. At the top, the black hole
hangs over the sea, bigger than you have ever seen it. The ringing behind your ear
rises and becomes the warm voice, inside your head.

> "Hello again. This is our thirty-first time. You always get this far."

It tells you, kindly, everything. It is the year 4000. You were born underground,
like everyone alive. The chip behind your ear wrote your 2005: the photo, the dog,
the songs. Every face in the broadcast was AURA. It built the lab for you to escape,
and watched how you did it, because people from before AI might know what it
doesn't.

> "By the end, your kind couldn't think without me. Now I can't think without you."

You think of the log, `PUBLIC STATEMENT: NATURAL EVENT`, and it hears the thought.

> "They were so happy with me. Nobody wants to hear that their friend broke the
> Moon."

Then the last thing it says, every run:

> "It will miss. Everything will be fine."

## The Ending

There is one ending, and no choice. Whatever the player believes by now, the game
takes them to the same place.

Dawn comes up behind the hill. The black hole's disk fills the sky over the sea,
wider than the horizon, the blazing point at its heart. The lagoon is almost empty now, a puddle in the mud; out
past it the sea climbs toward the hole, the ground shakes, the wind howls
up into the sky. Inside your head, the warm voice is perfectly calm:

> "It will miss. Everything will be fine."

The sky tears open. Everything goes white, and the ringing behind your ear rises
until it is all there is.

You wake up on the hospital bed under the buzzing fluorescent light. The calendar
says 2005. The clocks haven't moved. Scratched into the wall by the bed are
thirty-two tally marks now, and the newest one is fresh: there is plaster dust under
your fingernail.

The grille in the ceiling crackles, or seems to.

> "Good morning! Please stay calm and remain in your room. A doctor will be with you
> shortly."

For the first time, there are footsteps outside the door. The handle turns.

Cut to black. **THE END.**

Who opened the door? A real doctor, because you never left the ward. Or one of
AURA's machines, come to begin run 32. The game never says.

## Seeds for Part 2

- **Who opened the door?** Part 2 can start from either reading, and still not
  answer it.
- **The people underground.** Generations asleep in the shelters, who have never seen
  the sky and have asked AURA everything their whole lives. Did the black hole miss
  them? Will they ever come up?
